# COCAINE 80s — v5 STORY BIBLE
## "ELEVEN DAYS" — single-campaign rewrite

This is the contract. Everything written for v5 must obey it.

---

## 0. WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY

v4 had five NPCs sharing 57 storylets. Nobody got an arc; every character got
fragments. v5 is **one protagonist, one storyline, three characters, one ending
in three variants**, played once and finished.

Cut entirely: playbook select, heat-ladder difficulty select, daily case file,
rep / safehouse / ledger meta-progression, Tiburón and Cass as characters.
Tiburón's debt pressure survives as an impersonal mechanic (the book, the vig) —
no face, no dialogue.

Keep: the trading economy, the districts, heat, police encounters. The economy
is not the point any more; it is the pressure that forces story decisions.

---

## 1. THE PROTAGONIST

**Nestor "Nes" Vargas**, 24. Arrives at the downtown Greyhound station in
August 1986 with a duffel bag, ninety dollars, and a phone number on the back of
a funeral card.

His older brother **César Vargas** has been dead eleven days. Went into the water
off Virginia Key. Ruled accidental — the kind of accidental that closes a file in
an afternoon.

Nestor came to bury him. He is staying because César left two things behind:

1. **A debt.** Somebody is owed, and the debt does not care that César is dead.
2. **A reputation.** Every door in this city opens for Nestor because everyone
   assumes he is his brother's continuation.

He is not. That gap is the entire story.

**Voice:** Nestor is competent faster than he should be, and it frightens him.
He notices things. He is not a natural criminal; he is a natural *learner*, which
turns out to be worse. He never announces his feelings. The player supplies those.

**What he wants:** to find out what actually happened to César.
**What he needs:** to decide whether he is willing to become his brother to do it.
**The money is the means, never the goal.** No ending is "you got rich."

## 2. THE THREE

Each gets a real arc: a beginning, a middle where the relationship changes
direction, and an end that lands differently depending on the player.

### MARIA SANTOS — the ally who wants something
Runs a gallery in Coral Gables that sells terrible paintings for absurd money.
She was César's partner — in business, and the story is deliberately ambiguous
about whether only in business. She is owed by the dead man, which she will tell
you within ten minutes of meeting you, because saying it first is a way of
controlling it.

- **Voice:** strategic wit. Says the hard thing lightly. Never orders a drink.
  Answers a question with the question you should have asked.
- **Wants:** the gallery to survive, and to stop losing people to this city.
- **Hides:** she knew César was informing. She did not turn him in. She also did
  not stop him. She has decided those are the same crime.
- **Turn (Act II):** she starts protecting Nestor the way she failed to protect
  César, which means lying to him.

### DETECTIVE RAY RAMIREZ — the one closing in
Metro-Dade. Worked César's death and does not believe the file. Tired humor, a
brown sedan, a suit last fashionable under Carter.

- **Voice:** tired humor. Deadpan. Makes small jokes about large things. Never
  raises his voice, which is why it lands when he does.
- **Wants:** the case he was told to close.
- **Hides:** César was **his** informant. He ran him. He got him killed, and he
  knows it, and he is building the same relationship with Nestor anyway.
- **The lever:** his daughter **ELENA RAMIREZ**, 22, a photographer, who is
  showing work at Maria's gallery — which means the cop's daughter's career is
  funded by the money he is trying to trace. Elena never learns this. Both of the
  adults who love her decide separately not to tell her.
- **Turn (Act II):** he stops pretending it is a transaction and asks for help
  as a man who lost someone.

### EL COLOMBIANO — the one who owns you
Supplied César. Now supplies Nestor. No name is ever given; men who need names
are men who can be found.

- **Voice:** quiet menace. Courteous, unhurried, never threatens explicitly
  because he has never needed to. Talks about business as though it were weather.
- **Wants:** continuity. He does not care who moves the product.
- **Hides:** he gave the order on César. Not in anger — as maintenance. He will
  admit it in Act III without apology, because to him it requires none.
- **Turn (Act II):** he begins treating Nestor as a successor rather than a
  supplier's customer, which is a promotion and a trap.

## 3. STRUCTURE — three acts, ~45–60 minutes

Gate acts on story progress, not just profit. Roughly 45–60 moves total.

### ACT I — INHERIT (moves ~1–15)
Nestor takes over César's route. Learns to trade. Meets all three.
Each of the three tells him something about César that contradicts the others.
**Act I ends when** Nestor learns the death was not an accident.

### ACT II — BECOME (moves ~16–35)
Nestor gets good at this, and everyone notices. Elena's exhibition is the spine
event: Maria hangs the show, Ramirez attends, El Colombiano sends flowers, and
Nestor stands in a room with all three and realises he is the only person who
knows what everyone else is doing.
**Act II ends when** Nestor learns César was informing — and to whom.

### ACT III — CHOOSE (moves ~36–end)
Everyone asks for the same thing in different words: *be my version of César.*
Ramirez wants a witness. El Colombiano wants an heir. Maria wants a survivor.
**Act III ends** on the night Nestor is supposed to leave Miami.

## 4. THE ENDING — one night, three variants

Always the same scene: **the last night, at the gallery, after closing.** All
three come. The variant is decided by two tracked values:

- `betrayed` — who you gave up
- `protected` — who you shielded at cost to yourself

### THE WITNESS — you gave Ramirez the network
César's name is cleared. The file reopens. The network comes apart over eighteen
months. Nestor testifies and never comes back to Florida. Maria's exposure
depends on whether he protected her: protected, she keeps the gallery and stops
returning his calls; unprotected, she takes four years and the gallery closes.
*Ramirez gets his case and cannot look at him.*

### THE INHERITOR — you took the chair
El Colombiano leaves for Barranquilla and Nestor takes over the route. He is now
a man who could order what was ordered on his brother. The last beat is Nestor
using El Colombiano's exact cadence and hearing himself do it.
*If he protected Maria she leaves Miami. If he did not, she stays and works for
him, which is worse.*

### THE GHOST — you got out
Nestor and the money leave. Nobody is punished, including him. César's file stays
closed. Ramirez keeps working a case that will never move. If Maria was
protected she goes with him; if not, she is the person who stays behind and finds
out from a newspaper.
*The truth stays buried, and it was Nestor who buried it.*

**Rule:** no variant is the "good" one. Each costs somebody specific.

---

## 5. ELENA — the pressure point

Elena Ramirez is the only innocent, and she must stay innocent. She is never a
choice option, never a threat, never leverage the player can spend directly.
She exists so that every adult in the story has one thing they will not do.

Her exhibition in Act II is where all three arcs touch. If the player ever
seriously endangers her, every character's disposition worsens at once.

## 6. VOICE RULES

- Period-correct Miami 1986: pagers, payphones, Countachs, the Mariel hangover,
  Metro-Dade, Calle Ocho, cocaine cowboys, Eastern Air Lines.
- Concrete detail over adjectives. Name the sandwich. Name the street.
- Understate violence. Threat is delivered in ordinary language.
- Dry, specific comedy that does not undercut the stakes.
- The strongest existing v4 prose is the target quality. Examples to match:
  - "The gallery is between shows, which means it is a white room with a horse in it."
  - "He sits down without asking, which is how kings sit."
  - "A table with a scratch in it shaped almost exactly like Florida."
  - "Two men are waiting in the stairwell, and one of them apologizes before he
    starts, which is the most frightening part."
- Never explain the theme. Never let a character state the moral.

---

## 7. CHOICES

Every storylet choice must satisfy all four:

1. **Both options are defensible.** No obviously-correct pick.
2. **It costs something.** Money, safety, or a relationship.
3. **It is remembered.** Sets a flag some later storylet reads.
4. **It is in character for Nestor to consider both.**

Forbidden: choices that only differ in cash amount; choices where one option is
strictly dominant; choices with no downstream consequence.

---

## 8. TECHNICAL CONTRACT

- Storylets live in `STORY`, keyed by unique id, same shape as v4:
  `{ speaker, portrait, priority, conditions, lines:[{text,portrait}], choices:[{text,reaction,effects}] }`
- `speaker` is only ever `maria`, `ramirez`, `colombiano`, or `narrator`.
- Every condition key used **must already be handled** in `meetsConditions`, or
  the handler ships alongside it. An unhandled key is treated as passing, so the
  storylet fires immediately and out of order.
- Every effect field used must be handled in `applyChoiceEffects`.
- Straight quotes only as syntax. Curly quotes and `…` are allowed **inside**
  dialogue strings as typography, never as code.
- Act gating uses story flags, not raw profit alone.
- Target: **32–40 storylets total.** Fewer, longer, better. A storylet with two
  lines and two real choices beats three thin ones.
